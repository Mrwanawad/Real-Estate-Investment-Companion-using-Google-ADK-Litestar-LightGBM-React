# Property Registration at the Real Estate Registry (Shahr El-Akary), synthetic guide

## Why register
A primary sale contract gives the buyer a contractual right only. Registration at the Shahr El-Akary (real estate registry) makes the ownership public and protects the buyer against a second sale of the same unit.

## When registration is possible
Registration is possible after the developer holds a registered title for the land and the unit has a final built area survey. In most projects this happens after handover, once all instalments are paid.

## Steps
The buyer collects the final contract, the payment clearance certificate, the unit survey and the national ID. The documents go to the registry office, the registry values the unit and the buyer pays the registration fee. The registry then issues the registered title.

## Costs and time
The synthetic registration fee is 0.5% of the unit value capped at EGP 2,000 plus a fixed stamp cost. Typical time is 2 to 4 months. Developers in this guide help with the paperwork for a service charge of EGP 5,000.

## Preliminary claim
A buyer without a final title may file a court sale validity claim, which is recorded and gives some protection. Ask a licensed lawyer before choosing this route.
